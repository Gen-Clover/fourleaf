# Gen Clover: Website Client Acquisition Strategy

*Prepared: 30 Sep 2026 · Updated: 30 Sep 2026 (pricing, care plans, internal tools, lead ID format, repo setup) · Goal: ₹3,00,000/month from website projects*

---

## 0. Where Gen Clover stands today

genclover.com is positioned as an AI, data and web-app engineering firm. Its three case studies are all AI/data projects in healthcare and publishing. That's a strength, but it means a clinic owner looking for a ₹40K website won't see anything that matches what they want to buy. Section 5 covers the fix.

---

## 1. What the targets mean in numbers

| Goal | Is it realistic? |
|---|---|
| **100+ websites a month** | Not in 10 days, and not at this price. 100 sites a month would need a team of 15–25 people and a sales operation behind it. At ₹30K per site, it's also a ₹30L/month business. That's a year-2 goal. |
| **₹3L a month (6–10 sites)** | **Doable.** The hard part is finding and closing clients, not building the sites. |
| **In 10 days** | In 10 days you can set up the whole system, contact 500+ businesses and close your **first 2–4 deals**. The full ₹3L month is realistic in **month 1–2**. |

To close 10 deals a month you need roughly this pipeline:

> ~1,000 personalised messages → ~80–100 replies → ~40 calls → **8–12 closed deals**. That's about 40–50 messages a day, which one person can do with the right tools.

## 2. What to sell: a fixed package

Clients agree faster when the price and scope are fixed. Sell a named package instead of "website development".

### Decided package prices

| Package | Price (+ 18% GST) | What's included | Care plan included in the quote |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | ₹30,000 | 5 pages, mobile, WhatsApp button, contact form, Google Business setup | Care Basic, ₹2,500/mo |
| **Growth** ⭐ | **₹40,000** | 8–10 pages, lead forms that send enquiries to email and WhatsApp, Google Sheet or CRM, basic SEO, blog, analytics, 7-day delivery | Care Plus, ₹3,500/mo |
| **Premium** | ₹50,000 | Growth plus custom design, booking/payments, landing pages for ads, speed and SEO audit (scope capped, see below) | Care Pro, ₹5,000/mo |

Things to settle before these go on the website:

- **Show them as "+ 18% GST".** Indian clients will be charged GST on top. If the site says "₹40,000" and the invoice says ₹47,200, the client will feel misled.
- **Premium at ₹50K is the one that can lose you money.** Write its scope down tightly (for example "up to 15 pages, 1 integration"). Otherwise it becomes a custom build at a template price.
- **Payment terms:** 50% upfront and 50% before go-live. The first care-plan month is billed at launch.
- **Offer yearly prepay at 10× the monthly fee.** You get the cash upfront and fewer clients drop off.
- **Domain renewal (about ₹1K a year) is billed to the client at cost.** Hosting is included in the care plan.

### Care plans: what maintenance actually includes

This work stops small problems from quietly costing the client leads:

| Task | Basic ₹2,500 | Plus ₹3,500 | Pro ₹5,000 |
|---|:-:|:-:|:-:|
| Hosting, SSL, domain renewal management | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Uptime monitoring with alerts | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Backups | Weekly | Weekly | Daily |
| Security and dependency updates (framework, plugins) | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| **Monthly test that the form, WhatsApp and email actually deliver leads** | ✅ | ✅ | ✅ |
| Content changes (text, images, prices, team) | 1 hr | 2 hrs | 4 hrs |
| Monthly report: visits, leads, top pages (GA4 + Search Console) | — | ✅ | ✅ |
| Search Console errors, broken links, sitemap | — | ✅ | ✅ |
| Google Business Profile posts | — | — | 2 a month |
| Quarterly speed and SEO check with fixes | — | — | ✅ |
| Response time | 48 h | 24 h | Same day |

Unused hours don't roll over. **Not included in any plan:** new pages, new features and redesigns. Those are enhancements (below).

The monthly lead-delivery test matters most. It's the check clients will value, because a broken contact form means enquiries silently go missing.

### Enhancement pricing (on-demand work)

**Hourly rate:** ₹1,000/hr, or ₹800/hr for care-plan clients, with a 2-hour minimum. Use $40–60/hr for US clients.

Clients prefer fixed prices, so also keep a menu:

| Enhancement | Price |
|---|---|
| Extra page (from template) | ₹3,000–4,000 |
| Ad landing page (Google or Meta) | ₹7,500–10,000 |
| New form or field, routed to Sheet or CRM | ₹2,500 |
| Analytics and pixel setup (GA4, Meta, conversion tracking) | ₹3,000 |
| Google Business Profile setup and optimisation | ₹3,000 |
| Booking (Cal.com or Calendly) | ₹5,000 |
| Razorpay payments | ₹8,000–12,000 |
| CRM integration (Zoho or HubSpot) | ₹8,000–15,000 |
| WhatsApp API or auto-reply bot | ₹10,000–15,000 + API costs |
| Second language (Hindi or Punjabi) | ₹1,500 per page |
| Speed and SEO audit with fixes | ₹7,500 |
| Rush delivery | +25% |

Selling care-plan clients the lower hourly rate also gives them a reason to stay on the plan.

### Why care plans matter

**The care plan matters most over time.** 30 clients on ₹3,000 a month is ₹90K of recurring income before you sell anything new. Every sale should include it.

**Make the pitch about leads.** Business owners don't buy "a website". They buy "more patient or customer enquiries on WhatsApp". Use that promise everywhere: *"A website that brings you enquiries on WhatsApp — live in 7 days."*

## 3. Who to target: businesses that already pay for leads

A good target already spends money getting customers, so ₹40K for a website looks cheap to them.

### India, starting from Chandigarh

1. **Immigration, study-abroad and IELTS consultants.** The tricity and Punjab have hundreds of them. They spend heavily on ads and each lead is worth a lot to them. **This is the best local niche.**
2. **Clinics:** dental, dermatology, IVF, physiotherapy, hair transplant.
3. **Real estate brokers and builders** in Mohali and Zirakpur. They need pages for individual projects.
4. **Hotels, homestays and resorts in Himachal.** They want direct bookings so they pay less commission to OTAs.
5. **Manufacturers and exporters** in Ludhiana, Mohali and Baddi. Many have very old websites and need export enquiries.
6. **Interior designers and architects.** Portfolio sites are what they sell with.
7. **CA firms, law firms, coaching institutes, premium gyms and salons.**

### USA (you need about 2 US clients to earn ₹3L)

- **Home services and local businesses:** roofers, HVAC, landscapers, cleaners, dentists, med spas, lawyers. They normally pay $1,500–$5,000 for a site, about ₹1.3–4L.
- **White-label work for US agencies:** agencies with more sites than they can build pay $800–2,000 per site. This is usually the fastest way to get US income because one partner can send you 3–5 sites a month.

## 5. Fix genclover.com before sending anyone there

Right now a prospect lands on an AI/data consultancy. Before outreach starts:

1. Add a **/websites page** with the three packages, "from ₹30,000 + GST", 7-day delivery and a FAQ.
2. Add a **WhatsApp click-to-chat button** and a short enquiry form. Your own site should show what you're selling.
3. Show **4–6 niche demo sites** (clinic, immigration consultant, real estate, hotel, interior designer, manufacturer) as a website portfolio. These also become the templates you build client sites from.
4. Set up **Google Business Profile** for "Gen Clover – Website Design Mohali/Chandigarh". People searching locally find agencies there.

## 6. Delivering 10 sites a month

- **One codebase with a theme per niche.** For example, Astro or Next.js with Tailwind (or WordPress if clients want to edit their own content), with ready sections: hero, services, testimonials, FAQ, WhatsApp, form, map.
- **Onboarding form** that collects logo, content, photos and domain in one go. Collecting content is where projects usually stall.
- Use AI to write the first draft of the copy, then edit it.
- **Payment terms: 50% upfront, 50% before go-live.** Hosting on Vercel or Hostinger is billed through your care plan.
- With this in place, one developer can deliver **8–12 sites a month.**

## 7. Tools to build

Each of these is a **self-contained tool** in the `tools\` folder of the **genclover-portal** repo (see section 10). They all run inside the one portal app, share one login and one database, and are linked by shared IDs.

0. **Client onboarding registration:** creates the unique Client ID (`GC-2026-0001`) and the client code (`ABR`) used everywhere in Gen Clover.
1. **Lead finder** (`tools/lead-finder`, built 30 Sep 2026): searches Google Maps by niche and area (a map grid gets past Google's 60-results-per-search limit), checks each business's website (missing site, social page only, down, no HTTPS, not mobile-friendly, slow, no WhatsApp, form or booking), scores every lead for six services (new website, redesign, WhatsApp & lead capture, Google Business Profile, SEO & speed, booking & payments), writes the first message from each lead's own findings, schedules follow-ups and converts won leads into clients. See `tools/lead-finder/README.md`.
2. **Demo generator:** turns each lead's name, services, photos and reviews into a personalised preview homepage in minutes.
3. **Outreach kit:** WhatsApp and email scripts, a follow-up sequence (day 1, 3, 7), Loom script, and objection answers ("too expensive", "my nephew makes sites").
4. **Pipeline tracker:** a simple CRM sheet or dashboard.
5. **Gen Clover /websites landing page** and the niche demo sites.

## 10. Internal tools: Gen Clover Portal

### Decision: one repo, one portal, tools as separate modules

`genclover-portal` is **one git repo and one portal application**. Each tool is a self-contained module inside it (a "modular monolith"):

```
genclover-portal\                  ← ONE git repo (npm workspaces)
├── apps\
│   ├── portal\                    ← the single website: login, sidebar, hosts every tool
│   │                                (financial-control-system becomes this; it already has
│   │                                 login, roles, Clients, AuditLog)
│   └── workers\                   ← background jobs (Lead Finder searches, website audits)
├── tools\                         ← one folder per tool, each self-contained
│   ├── finance\                   ← pages, actions, logic
│   ├── lead-finder\
│   ├── demo-generator\
│   └── outreach\
└── packages\                      ← shared by all tools
    ├── db\                        ← Prisma schema + client (User, Client, Lead, Sequence…)
    ├── auth\                      ← session, requireRole
    ├── ids\                       ← lead, client, project and invoice ID generators
    └── ui\                        ← shared components
```

Every tool sits behind one login, at URLs like `portal.genclover.com/finance`, `/leads` and `/demos`.

**Why not separately hosted microservices:** the tools depend on each other's data (Lead → Demo → Outreach → Pipeline → Client → Project → Invoice). With a 1–2 person team, separate services would mean building single sign-on, syncing copies of client data through APIs, and running several deployments before any tool is useful. **Why not one app with everything mixed in:** the code would get tangled as tools are added, and changing one tool would break others.

**Rules that keep the tools separate:**

1. A tool never imports code from another tool. Tools only use `packages/*`.
2. Each tool owns its own database tables. Shared tables (`User`, `Client`, `Sequence`) live in `packages/db`.
3. Anything long-running (Places searches, website audits, demo generation) runs in `apps/workers`, not inside a web request.

**When a tool gets split into its own service:** only if it needs a different language (for example a Python scraper or ML), heavy load, outside users (clients or partners), or stricter security isolation. Because each tool is already its own folder, splitting it out is a move, not a rewrite.

**Hosting:**
- **Portal:** Vercel, Railway or a small VPS.
- **Workers:** Railway, Render or a VPS, because they need a long-running process.
- **Database:** one MongoDB on Atlas, with the cluster in Mumbai (ap-south-1).
- **Public genclover.com:** stays a separate site and repo, and only links to the portal login.

**Look and feel:** the portal uses genclover.com's design: Inter and Chakra Petch fonts, the ink/silver palette with the clover-red accent, and the clover logo. It's dark by default with a light toggle. The theme lives in `packages/ui`, so every tool gets it automatically.

**Migration (done 30 Sep 2026):**
- `financial-control-system` became `apps/portal` (the shell) plus `tools/finance`. Its logic and flows are unchanged.
- `lead-generation` became `tools/lead-finder`, which is empty until its design is decided. Its docs moved to `docs/lead-generation/`.
- The empty `site/` folder was removed.

### IDs that travel everywhere

**Decided 30 Sep 2026, built in `packages/ids`.** Records link to each other by internal cuids. The readable IDs below are for people and the outside world. Once handed out they never change and are never reused.

| Record | Format | Set by | Created when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lead | `GL-YYYY-MM-XXXXXX` (e.g. `GL-2026-09-000001`) | System | The Lead Finder finds a business |
| Client ID | `GC-YYYY-NNNN` (e.g. `GC-2026-0001`) | System, year of registration (IST) | The lead converts at onboarding. The lead record links to it, so its history carries over. |
| Client code | 2–10 letters/digits starting with a letter (e.g. `ABR` for Abrams Books) | The person onboarding the client. Locked afterwards. | At onboarding |
| Project | `<client code>-Pnn` (e.g. `ABR-P01`) | System, numbered per client | A project starts |
| Care plan | A project of type "Care plan" (e.g. `ABR-P03`) | System | The site goes live on a care plan |
| Invoice | `GCI/26-27/0001` | System, when issued | One consecutive series per Indian financial year, as GST requires. At most 16 characters. Drafts have no number yet. |
| Payment | Bank UTR / FIRC reference | Bank | Recorded against the invoice |

The client code goes into Jira (it's the Jira project key), folder and repo names, the WhatsApp group name and email subjects. Invoices show the Client ID, client code and project ID.

Numbers come from atomic counters in the shared `Sequence` table, never from counting existing rows. `npm run db:push` also backfills any record that is missing its ID.

**Lead ID rules (`GL-YYYY-MM-XXXXXX`):**

- `GL` = Gen Clover Lead, always stored in uppercase. Searching is case-insensitive.
- `YYYY-MM` is the month the lead was **first found**, using the IST date. It never changes, even if the lead converts in a later month.
- `XXXXXX` is a counter that **restarts at 000001 each month**. It's padded to at least 6 digits. If a month ever passes 999,999 it grows to 7 digits instead of failing.
- The next number comes from a per-month counter that is incremented atomically. It is never worked out by counting existing leads, which can produce duplicates.
- One business gets only one lead ID. Duplicates are removed using the Google Place ID, so re-running a search doesn't create new IDs.

**Database:** all tools share **one MongoDB database** through `packages/db`, which the portal and any background workers both use. Each tool keeps its tables in its own schema file (`packages/db/prisma/schema/<tool>.prisma`).

## 11. Local directory and repo setup

### What's there now

- The code is spread across folders named after old purposes, such as "Gen Clover Calculator/genclover-portal".
- There are manual backup folders (`*-git-backup`, `genclover-main.zip`). A private GitHub remote replaces these.
- Client documents (`eligo/`) sit next to code folders.
- Paths contain spaces (`Welcome to 2026`). Node and git tools sometimes have trouble with that.

### Recommended layout

Keep documents and code separate, and give code a short path without spaces:

```
D:\GenClover\
├── 00-company\         ← Centralised Documents (governance, registration, expenses)
├── 01-brand\           ← logos, Digital Content, LinkedIn, Clovi
├── 02-sales\           ← strategy docs, proposal and pitch templates
├── 03-clients\
│   └── GC-2026-0001-crb-eligo\   ← contracts, SOW, assets (named by client ID)
├── 99-archive\         ← old backups and zips (kept, just out of the way)
└── code\               ← git repos only
    ├── genclover-portal\    ← ONE repo: portal app + all internal tools (section 10)
    │   ├── apps\  (portal, workers)
    │   ├── tools\ (finance, lead-finder, …)
    │   └── packages\ (db, auth, ids, ui)
    ├── genclover-website\   ← public genclover.com
    ├── site-starter\        ← niche templates (GitHub "template repo")
    └── client-sites\
        └── gc-2026-0002-drsharma-dental\   ← one repo per client site
```

### How the repos are organised

| Repo | Why it's separate |
|---|---|
| **genclover-portal** (one repo: portal + all internal tools) | The tools share one login, one database and the same lead and client IDs. Each tool is still kept separate in its own `tools\` folder (section 10). |
| **site-starter** | Every client site is created from it, so a fix to a template carries into new sites. |
| **One repo per client site** | Each has its own deploys, can be handed over cleanly if a client leaves, and failures stay isolated. |

All repos should be **private on a GitHub organisation** (for example `genclover`), not a personal account. Then the backup folders can go.
